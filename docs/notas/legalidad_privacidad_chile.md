# Legal and privacy constraints on recording meetings with AI tools from Chile (as of 2 November 2026)

Provenance tags: **[P]** official text read directly (statute, bill, court order, regulator or vendor's own policy). **[P-excerpt]** a court ruling read only through excerpts reproduced by a third party. **[C]** commentary or press, opened and read. **[S]** seen only in a search-result summary or snippet, page not opened or blocked, so lower confidence. Vendor and press material is paraphrased. Only legal texts and rulings are quoted. This is research for a decision, not legal advice. Research stopped when the session's web-search limit was reached, and the affected items are flagged under Gaps.

## 1. Chile: Article 161-A, participant versus third party, and whether a bot changes the analysis

### Takeaway
Article 161-A punishes recording private conversations in non-public places "sin autorización del afectado", by any means, and its text has no exception for participants. The Supreme Court's best-known ruling (2013, Dra. Cordero) says a participant does not commit the offense, but the case law is not uniform: a 2007 ruling and a 2016 ruling upheld convictions for hidden recordings, and a 2023 ruling said a covert participant recording could fit the offense. So a third party recording is plainly covered, a participant recording with a clear OK is safe, and a covert participant recording is arguable. I found no Chilean authority on AI notetakers.

### Cited Findings

**Primary text**
- [P] Art. 161-A inc. 1: "Se castigará con la pena de reclusión menor en cualquiera de sus grados y multa de 50 a 500 Unidades Tributarias Mensuales al que, en recintos particulares o lugares que no sean de libre acceso al público, sin autorización del afectado y por cualquier medio, capte, intercepte, grabe o reproduzca conversaciones o comunicaciones de carácter privado [...]" — [Código Penal, BCN/Ley Chile](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1984)
- [P] Inc. 2 applies the same penalty to whoever "difunda" that material. Inc. 3 raises it (reclusión menor en su grado máximo, 100 to 500 UTM) when the same person obtained and disclosed it. Inc. 4 exempts those authorized by law or court order. — [Código Penal](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1984)
- [P] Art. 25 sets temporal "menores" penalties at 61 days to 5 years — [Código Penal](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1984). With the November 2026 UTM at $72,440, the fine range of 50 to 500 UTM is about CLP 3.6M to 36.2M (my calculation) — [SII](https://www.sii.cl/valores_y_fechas/utm/utm2026.htm)
- [P] Art. 161-B punishes using those acts to demand money or conduct (a coercion offense) — [Código Penal](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1984)
- [P] Constitution Art. 19 N° 5 protects "la inviolabilidad del hogar y de toda forma de comunicación privada" — [Constitución](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=242302)
- [P] Ley 21.459 Art. 3 separately punishes whoever "indebidamente intercepte, interrumpa o interfiera, por medios técnicos, la transmisión no pública de información en un sistema informático" — [Ley 21.459](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1177743)

**Rulings, in order**
- [C] 2007, Chilevisión I (juez Calvo), Corte Suprema Rol 3005-2006, 9 Aug 2007: a third party coordinated with journalists filmed his own conversation with a judge. Bascuñán reports five coincident decisions, ending with this one, that read 161-A as punishing covert recording by an interlocutor — [Bascuñán, Revista de Ciencias Penales, 2014](https://revistadecienciaspenales.cl/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Ciencias-Penales-3.pdf)
- [C] 2013, Chilevisión II (Dra. Cordero), Corte Suprema Rol 8393-2012, 21 Aug 2013: a 3-2 acquittal of TV journalists who posed as patients in a psychiatrist's private office, using a hidden camera and microphone. The Court said only a third party outside the conversation intrudes, and that someone who reveals facts to another "renuncia a su expectativa de intimidad", save for professional secrecy. It also relied on public interest. Dissemination was then not punishable. The first-instance court had held the opposite, that the statute does not distinguish — [Bascuñán](https://revistadecienciaspenales.cl/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Ciencias-Penales-3.pdf)
- [P-excerpt] 2016, "En su propia trampa", Corte Suprema Segunda Sala, Rol 38159-2016 (unanimous): nullity rejected on the merits, so convictions under 161-A stood (61 days, fines of 10, 6 and 4 UTM) for a TV team that covertly recorded a woman in her home. Key lines: authorization "lo es para grabar el acto o comunicación, y no para ingresar al recinto"; "Al poner su confianza en la persona equivocada, no autorizó, en modo alguno, que sus palabras [...] fueran grabadas subrepticiamente"; "La autorización exigida por el tipo penal en estudio debe ser inequívoca". The Court cited Rol 8393-2012 for the public-interest point and said it had no bearing on the authorization issue — [Microjuris](https://aldiachile.microjuris.com/2016/08/16/corte-suprema-acoge-parcialmente-recurso-de-nulidad-de-equipo-periodistico-condenado-por-grabacion-ilegal/)
- [P-excerpt] 2018, Dirección del Trabajo v. BCI, Corte Suprema Cuarta Sala, Rol 35.159-2017, 12 Apr 2018 (unification of labor case law): an attendee secretly recorded a meeting. Admitted as evidence because the speaker's expectation of privacy was not objectively reasonable, and exclusion is limited to evidence obtained by violating fundamental rights. The majority did not discuss 161-A. The dissent (abogado integrante Correa) invoked it. The appeal relied on Rol 30-2016 (Valdivia), which admitted a worker's recording of a company meeting — [Derechopedia, text of ruling](https://derechopedia.cl/Unificaci%C3%B3n_-_Fallo_BCI,_Grabaciones_y_Prueba_Il%C3%ADcita_-_Rol_N%C2%B0_35.159-2017)
- [C] 2023, Codelco executive: a senior executive who led a meeting and recorded it without telling attendees was dismissed. The Santiago appeals court called that an anomalous conduct, and the Supreme Court let the dismissal stand — [Meganoticias](https://www.meganoticias.cl/nacional/403647-filtracion-de-audio-ejecutivo-codelco-despedido-grabar-sin-consentimiento-pdp-26-1-2023.html). Per a labor firm's summary, the Supreme Court said the conduct could fit 161-A because no public interest was involved, while also citing the earlier line finding no offense when an interlocutor discloses. The rol and full text were not available — [AZ](https://www.az.cl/es-valido-un-despido-por-grabar-reunion-de-trabajo-sin-autorizacion/)
- [C] Live test case, Caso Audio: criminal law scholars said in 2023 that, following the Cordero line, a recording by someone present would not be an offense — [Radio U. de Chile](https://radio.uchile.cl/?p=752847). On 13 Oct 2026 the court rejected the defense requests, including a separate hearing on the audio's legality, with no reasoning reported. Trial preparation is set for 1 Mar 2027 — [Emol](https://www.emol.com/noticias/Nacional/2026/10/13/1214009/luis-hermosilla-caso-audios.html). [S] Hermosilla also filed a criminal complaint against the participant who recorded him — [El Ciudadano](https://www.elciudadano.com/chile/asegura-que-fue-un-montaje-hermosilla-presenta-querella-contra-leonarda-villalobos-por-grabarlo-sin-su-consentimiento/02/06/)
- [S] A separate route exists: in a recurso de protección (Rol 40.515-2025) the Supreme Court ordered a broadcaster to delete footage obtained inside a home without consent — [DOE](https://actualidadjuridica.doe.cl/corte-suprema-ordeno-a-megamedia-eliminar-las-grabaciones-obtenidas-al-interior-del-domicilio-del-recurrente-sin-su-consentimiento/)

**Commentary**
- [C] Bascuñán treats the interlocutor argument as the better reading of the 2013 holding. He calls the restrictive reading prudent, but argues the literal text covers a participant who records without the other's knowledge, and that the broader reading carries more weight. This is a scholar's criticism, not law — [Bascuñán](https://revistadecienciaspenales.cl/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Ciencias-Penales-3.pdf)
- [C] A 2018 Colegio de Abogados column criticized the BCI ruling and argued that secret recording in a private setting remains covered by 161-A — [Colegio de Abogados](https://colegioabogados.cl/fin-no-justifica-los-medios/)

### Inferences
- **Three-tier risk.** (a) Recording a meeting you are not in, or letting a bot attend without you: clearly the third-party case, so do not do it. (b) Participant with notice and a clear yes: "autorización del afectado" is present, lowest risk. (c) Participant recording covertly: arguable, probably not criminal under the 2013 line outside any confidentiality duty, but not safe given 2007, 2016 and 2023. It may also cost someone their job even without a conviction.
- **The 2013 rule may be narrower than often stated.** The 2016 Court read that case mainly through public interest, and all three penal cases involved journalism. I found no Supreme Court criminal ruling on a participant quietly recording an ordinary private business meeting.
- **A bot does not change the statute, but it changes the facts.** "Por cualquier medio" is technology-neutral. A bot sent by a participant who is present is the closest to the participant's own device, though a vendor that keeps the data for its own purposes looks more like a third party. A US court reasoned this way in the Otter case (section 5), which is persuasive at most. A bot auto-joining while its owner is absent has no admitted interlocutor behind it, which is the highest risk. A visible bot is not by itself authorization, given the 2016 requirement of "inequívoca" authorization.
- **Bot-free capture is not safer.** It removes the visible signal, so unannounced use looks more like "grabación subrepticia", not less.
- **Consent to record is not consent to circulate.** Sending transcripts around or publishing clips can raise the separate "difunda" limb if the recording was unauthorized, and the data-law purpose limit applies regardless. Whether uploading to a processor is "difusión" is undecided. I think it is unlikely, but this is unsettled.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain full rulings from the Poder Judicial, only excerpts and a law review. A lawyer should pull Rol 8393-2012, Rol 38159-2016 and the 2023 ruling, whose rol I could not find.
- The 2016 report does not say outright that the recorders spoke with the victim. Its wording strongly suggests it. A UChile paper on interlocutors under 161-A was blocked by the repository.
- I found no Chilean ruling or law-firm commentary on AI notetakers under 161-A, and none on whether a video call meets "recintos particulares o lugares que no sean de libre acceso al público". I assume it does when people connect from offices or homes.

## 2. Chile: Ley 19.628, Ley 21.719 and the labor context

### Takeaway
As of today the legal date for Ley 21.719 is still 1 December 2026, but the Senate passed a bill on 28 October moving it to **15 October 2027**, and the bill is now in the Chamber with "discusión inmediata". Check again before publishing. Once in force, the new law treats a meeting recording as personal data, voiceprints as biometric sensitive data, and a business as a responsible party, with no SME exemption. The Dirección del Trabajo has no opinion I could find on recording meetings or AI notetakers, but its audiovisual-control doctrine sets the framework.

### Cited Findings

**Entry into force**
- [P] Art. primero transitorio: the amendments take effect "el día primero del mes vigésimo cuarto posterior a la publicación", which gives 1 Dec 2026 from the 13 Dec 2024 version date — [Ley 21.719](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1209272). BCN already serves a consolidated Ley 19.628 dated 2026-12-01 — [Ley 19.628, future version](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-12-01)
- [P] Boletín 18.623-07: entered 1 Sep 2026; approved in general and in particular on 28 Oct 2026; sent to the Chamber on 29 Oct (oficio 355-26). Status read today: "Segundo trámite constitucional (C.Diputados)", "En tramitación", urgency "Discusión inmediata", no law number — [Senado, tramitación](https://tramitacion.senado.cl/wspublico/tramitacion.php?boletin=18623)
- [P] The Senate text replaces the date with "15 de octubre de 2027", expands the Agency board to five, and requires first appointments at least nine months before entry into force. It does not touch Art. sexto transitorio — [Oficio del Senado](http://www.senado.cl/appsenado/index.php?mo=tramitacion&ac=getDocto&iddocto=37381&tipodoc=ofic)
- [C] The vote was 33 to 6. The Executive had asked for 1 Dec 2027. Earlier articles citing that date are out of date — [Emol](https://www.emol.com/noticias/Economia/2026/10/28/1215480/ley-datos-personales-senado-postergacion.html). The Senate rejected the first board slate in May, the appointment deadline lapsed in June, and the Constitution Committee rejected extending the SME warning to all companies — [Diario Financiero](https://www.df.cl/senado-aprueba-modificaciones-al-proyecto-que-posterga-la-entrada-en)

**What the new law says** (all [P], [consolidated Ley 19.628 as amended](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-12-01))
- Scope: Art. 1 covers natural and legal persons but excludes "el tratamiento de datos que efectúen las personas naturales en relación con sus actividades personales". Art. 1 bis covers controllers established in Chile.
- Personal data: Art. 2 f) is any information linked to an identified or identifiable natural person.
- Biometrics: Art. 16 ter defines them as data "obtenidos a partir de un tratamiento técnico específico" that "permitan o confirmen la identificación única", naming "la voz". They need the express consent of Art. 16 ("declaración escrita, verbal o por un medio tecnológico equivalente") plus notice of the system, purpose, period and how to exercise rights.
- Sensitive data: Art. 2 g) includes health and political or union affiliation, among others.
- Consent: Art. 12 requires it to be "libre, informado y específico", prior and "inequívoca", by verbal or written statement, electronic means or an affirmative act. It is revocable, and the controller must prove it. Consent is presumed not free when collected as part of a contract that does not require that collection.
- Other bases: Art. 13 includes contract, legitimate interest (if rights are not affected) and the defense of rights in court.
- Purpose: Art. 3 b) bars uses other than those informed at collection, with limited exceptions.
- Notice: the information duty includes transfers abroad and retention period (Art. 14 ter h and i).
- Processors: Art. 15 bis says a processor acts on instructions, may not use data for another purpose, and is governed by a contract.
- Transfers: Art. 27 allows them to adequate countries, under clauses or binding rules with adequate guarantees, or under certification. Without these, a transfer that is "específica y que no sea habitual" may rely on express consent or contract necessity. Art. 28 has the Agency decide adequate countries and approve model clauses. A transfer in breach is a serious infraction (Art. 34 ter m).
- Anonymization: Art. 2 k) defines it as an "irreversible" procedure.
- Fines: Art. 35 sets light at warning or up to 5,000 UTM, serious up to 10,000, very serious up to 20,000 (about CLP 362M, 724M and 1,449M at the [November UTM](https://www.sii.cl/valores_y_fechas/utm/utm2026.htm), my calculation).

**Small business**
- [P] No exemption. Art. 14 septies lets the Agency set differentiated information and security standards by company size. Art. 37 counts economic capacity, and the revenue-based 2% or 4% repeat-offender fine excludes "empresas de menor tamaño" — [Ley 19.628, future version](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-12-01)
- [P] Art. sexto transitorio: for the first 12 months the Agency "podrá" impose a written warning on those companies. This is discretionary and not a grace period — [Ley 21.719](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1209272)

**Law in force today**
- [P] Ley 19.628 applies to data "en registros o bancos de datos". Art. 4 requires legal authorization or express consent, which must be informed and "constar por escrito". Art. 10 restricts sensitive data. Art. 23 allows damages, including moral damages — [Ley 19.628, current version](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-11-02)

**Labor**
- [P] Código del Trabajo: Art. 5 limits employer powers by constitutional guarantees, especially "la intimidad, la vida privada o la honra". Art. 154 says "toda medida de control" must use suitable means and be general and impersonal. Art. 154 ter requires confidentiality of workers' private data (DT opinions still cite it as 154 bis). Art. 453 denies evidentiary value to proof obtained by illicit means or by violating fundamental rights — [Código del Trabajo](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=207436)
- [P] ORD. 2328/130 (19 Jul 2002): audiovisual control is lawful only for technical-productive or safety reasons, with monitoring of the worker secondary or accidental. It must be in the internal regulations, and workers get access to recordings of themselves. The request mentioned sound, but the analysis addressed cameras — [DT](https://www.dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-63600.html)
- [P] ORD. 3877 (13 Aug 2019): permanent control is a disproportionate attack on privacy — [DT](https://www.dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-117382.html)
- [P] ORD. 2927/58 (28 Dec 2021): requiring a teleworker to keep webcam or audio on all day is not lawful — [DT](https://www.dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-121648.html)
- [P] ORD. 798 (26 Nov 2024): monitoring must not be clandestine, recordings need safe custody and deletion within a reasonable time, altering them is unlawful, and other uses are generally not allowed — [DT](https://dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-127042.html)
- [P] ORD. 2000-49883/2026 (7 Aug 2026), the closest analogue: AI facial analysis of drivers is judged by purpose, suitability, necessity and proportionality, is high intensity, needs reinforced safeguards as biometric data, and must be in the internal regulations. The DT does not pre-authorize such systems — [DT](https://www.dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-129528.html)

### Inferences
- Criminal and data law are separate tests. Legitimate interest under Art. 13 might support business notes under data law, but it does not supply the "autorización del afectado" of 161-A. Asking for consent answers both.
- Plain audio or text is personal data, not automatically biometric. Speaker recognition that builds voice profiles likely falls under Art. 16 ter, so treat it as needing express consent or turn it off.
- A person keeping private notes may fall under the "actividades personales" exclusion. A company, freelancer or employee acting for work likely does not.
- A vendor that trains on recordings for itself does not fit the Art. 15 bis processor role cleanly. For business use, prefer a plan with no training and a contract. "De-identified" vendor data should not be assumed to meet the "irreversible" anonymization test.
- Routine use of a foreign tool does not fit the one-off consent route in Art. 27, so it needs contractual guarantees, and no Agency-approved clauses or country list exist yet. This is unsettled.
- Today's Art. 4 asks for written consent, so a "sí" typed in the chat is better evidence than a spoken one under either regime. Whether one recording is a "banco de datos" is uncertain, but a searchable transcript library looks closer to one.
- Employers should treat recording of staff as a control measure: known, general, proportionate, in the Reglamento Interno, with no disciplinary reuse. Consent from an employee may not be "libre". Workers who record: BCI helps with evidence, and Codelco shows the job risk. Both need a lawyer, as do union meetings.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm a Chamber vote date or outcome. Re-check the [Senate page](https://tramitacion.senado.cl/wspublico/tramitacion.php?boletin=18623) before the report goes out.
- I found no DT opinion on recording meetings, audio-only capture or AI notetakers, and no Agency guidance, since the Agency's board is not yet constituted.
- I did not check the DT-cited 2025 opinions (ORD. 584, ORD. 388/32), and the latter is listed as superseding earlier doctrine where incompatible.

## 3. Participants abroad

### Takeaway
Treat a call as subject to the strictest law among its participants. In practice, announcing and getting a yes from everyone covers the US all-party states, the EU and Brazil on the recording question, though not every data-protection duty.

### Cited Findings
- [C] US: federal law generally needs one party's consent (18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d)). Mayer Brown lists California, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington as all-party states — [Mayer Brown, June 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk). [C] Lists differ at the edges: a vendor guide names nine firm states (those above minus Connecticut, Michigan and Nevada) and treats Connecticut, Delaware, Michigan, Nevada and Oregon as mixed or unsettled — [Scribbl, 12 Oct 2026](https://scribbl.co/post/do-you-have-to-tell-people-youre-recording)
- [P] California Penal Code § 632: recording a "confidential communication" without the consent of all parties is punishable, and the definition excludes settings where parties could reasonably expect to be overheard or recorded — [§ 632](https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_632). § 637.2 gives $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages, with no need to show actual damages — [§ 637.2](https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_637.2)
- [C] Illinois BIPA requires written consent for voiceprints — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk)
- [P] GDPR Art. 3 reaches non-EU controllers when offering goods or services to people in the EU or monitoring their behavior there — [GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/). [C] Mayer Brown says GDPR likely applies if participants are in Europe, national laws may add consent or criminal rules, and employment use may require works council involvement — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk)
- [P] Germany: § 201 StGB punishes unauthorized sound recording of another's non-public spoken word with up to three years or a fine — [§ 201 StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__201.html)
- [P] Brazil: STF Tema 237 (RE 583937) holds that a recording by one interlocutor without the other's knowledge is lawful evidence — [Tema 237](https://tesesesumulas.com.br/tese/stf/237). LGPD Art. 4 I excludes processing for exclusively private, non-economic purposes — [LGPD](https://lgpd-brazil.info/chapter_01/article_04). [C] Transcription-only use may be general personal data, while voiceprints raise obligations. Mayer Brown advises Portuguese-language notices and ANPD-approved transfer tools — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk)

### Inferences
- Under Art. 3(2)'s text, one EU attendee on a Chilean firm's call does not clearly trigger GDPR unless the firm offers services there or monitors behavior. Mayer Brown takes the more cautious practical view, and I would follow it for client calls. National criminal rules like § 201 may apply regardless.
- Brazil's Tema 237 is about evidence and does not cover the data-law duties of a business using a notetaker.

### Gaps
- No primary regulator guidance from EU authorities or Brazil's ANPD on AI notetakers surfaced. Planalto's LGPD page blocked access, so the full LGPD text was not read. Which state's law applies to a cross-border call was not researched.

## 4. Data handling by Claude

### Takeaway
Consumer plans (Free, Pro, Max, and now Ultra) can train on chats only under the "Help improve" setting, with 5-year retention when on. Commercial plans (Team, Enterprise, API) do not train by default, and both have exceptions for feedback and safety flags. Raw connector content is excluded from training, but it is stored with the chat on Anthropic's servers.

Anthropic's pages do not state it, so check the toggle.
- [P] Covered Models (Mythos-class, and Fable): since 9 Jun 2026, 30-day retention even for customers otherwise on ZDR, with human review only through a logged, controlled path. Consumer plans are unaffected — [Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models). [S] A secondary blog surfaced in search claimed API logs dropped to 7 days in 2025. Anthropic's own July 2026 page says 30 days, and I rely on that — [Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)
- [P] On Bedrock and Google-managed Claude, the cloud provider is the processor — [API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)
- [P] Non-users: the Non-User Privacy Policy (8 Jul 2026) names Anthropic PBC as controller outside Europe, says inputs may enter training through feedback, safety flags or opt-in, and offers requests via privacy@anthropic.com. It does not allocate responsibility for third parties' data in user inputs — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/non-user-privacy-policy)

**How data flows through a connector**
- [P] Claude connects to a remote connector from Anthropic's cloud, not your device, brokered through your account, and only reaches what you authorized. Custom connectors are not verified by Anthropic — [Custom connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp)
- [P] Retrieved data is stored on Anthropic's servers with the chat, and deleting the chat deletes it. Anthropic does not train on Gmail, Drive or Calendar connector data, but on consumer plans with training allowed, pasted content may be used — [Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- [P] Consumer training excludes raw connector content, including remote and local MCP, unless copied into the chat — [Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023580-is-my-data-used-for-model-training)
- [P] On the API, the MCP connector is not covered by ZDR and follows standard retention — [API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)
- [S] The Microsoft 365 connector fetches on demand without caching files, but results in saved chats remain — [M365 security guide](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12684923-microsoft-365-connector-security-guide)

### Inferences
- A transcript pulled through a connector ends up in at least two places, the notetaker and the Anthropic chat, each with its own retention, so deletion must happen in both. The data also goes to foreign providers, which should be mentioned in the notice.
- Business use fits Team, Enterprise or API better, because of the no-training term and DPA. On Pro or Max, keep the toggle off and delete chats, and remember the toggle does not make the contract a processor agreement.
- On any plan, avoid thumbs up or down on chats containing meeting content, since that can keep the whole conversation for 5 years.
- For Chilean legal privilege, no authority surfaced. [C] In the US, *United States v. Heppner* (S.D.N.Y., Feb 2026) denied privilege for materials prepared with consumer Claude, per [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk). Keep legal meetings out unless counsel directs otherwise.

### Gaps
- I did not read Anthropic's DPA for its transfer mechanism, or the current Privacy Policy. I could not confirm from primary text whether the July 2026 policy update changed anything; the Privacy Center pages dated after it are consistent with the table.
- I found no statement about where Chilean users' data is stored. I assume the US.

## 6. Etiquette: bot blocking and how notifications differ

### Takeaway
A bot may simply be refused now: Teams holds detected bots in the lobby by default and Google Meet's restricted access denies them. Bot-based tools are visible but not necessarily understood. Bot-free tools are invisible unless you turn on or give a notice yourself. Either way, the host still has to tell people.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Teams: the default policy detects external bots and holds them in the lobby regardless of lobby settings until explicitly admitted. Some bots go undetected and humans can be misclassified — [Microsoft Learn, updated 20 Jul 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/manage-external-bots)
- [C] Teams: an auto-block option (MC1459141) was scheduled for general availability during October 2026, off by default, per [Scribbl, 29 Oct 2026](https://scribbl.co/post/microsoft-teams-blocking-meeting-bots-2026). Timing differs from [UC Today](https://uctoday.com/microsoft-teams-to-block-external-bots-automatically-as-ai-notetaker-crackdown-hardens), which said August, and Microsoft's Learn page did not yet list it. [S] The cmdlet lists `BlockDetectedBots` — [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/microsoftteams/set-csteamsmeetingpolicy?view=teams-ps)
- [S] Google Meet: under restricted access, bots that ask to join are denied automatically — [Google](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/11989526?hl=en). Admins can require all participants to consent for "Take notes for me" — [Google](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/14754931?hl=en)
- [S] Zoom: participants get a recording consent prompt when a recording starts or when they join one, and it is required for guests — [Zoom](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0059819). A custom AI notice can hold mic and camera until accepted — [Zoom](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0082011). Vendor guides say there is no blanket bot switch, only per-app controls and the waiting room — [Reline](https://www.reline.so/blog/how-to-block-ai-notetakers-from-your-meetings)
- [P] Institutions: Virginia Tech (11 Mar 2026) names Otter, Read and Fireflies as unauthorized, approves the built-in Zoom and Teams assistants, tells hosts to remove others, and said it would block them on managed systems — [Virginia Tech](https://news.vt.edu/notices/it/2026/03/it-ai-meeting-assistants.html). [S] Eastern Washington blocked read.ai and otter.ai integrations for lack of data agreements — [EWU](https://support.ewu.edu/support/solutions/articles/10000099209). York University allows only approved assistants for internal meetings — [York](https://www.yorku.ca/uit/cio/wp-content/uploads/sites/936/2026/02/Guidelines-AI-Chatbots-Assistants.pdf)
- [C] Bot-based versus bot-free: Fathom says bot-based tools trigger a notice on the call, while bot-free tools capture device audio, show nothing in the attendee list and need no admission, and that the disclosure duty stays with the host. It offers a Mac bot-free beta — [Fathom, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.fathom.ai/learn/bot-free-note-taker). This is a vendor's own framing.
- [C] Granola offers an optional chat message and video watermark on Zoom, Meet and Teams, with no built-in notice for in-person or phone calls. It describes these as notice rather than agreement and recommends asking aloud — [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/blog/ai-notetaker-participant-privacy-consent). The complaint alleges both default to off — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/what-the-granola-class-action-means-for-companies-building-and-deploying-conversation-capture-tools)

### Inferences
- Good practice: ask the organizer first when it is someone else's meeting, because their policy decides whether a bot gets in. Do not send a bot alone. Announce it even with a visible bot. Stop if anyone objects. Repeat the notice when someone joins late. Share the notes with attendees. Using the platform's own recording, when you host, also triggers its native indicator.
- Using a bot-free tool to get around a host's block would be poor etiquette and would weaken any claim of authorization.

### Gaps
- Claims of a March 2026 Google Meet crackdown on notetaker bots appear only on vendor blogs. I could not confirm them in Google's own posts. Corporate bans are reported only secondhand, and I found no Chilean company or institution policy.

## 7. Rule of thumb and consent line in Chilean Spanish

### Takeaway
Rule of thumb: **"Si no estás en la reunión, no se graba. Si estás, se avisa antes, se pide un sí claro de cada persona y, si alguien dice que no, no se graba."** Then send only what is needed to the AI, on a no-training setting, and delete it when done.

### Cited Findings
The wording is my drafting, built to meet these requirements:
- [P] "Autorización del afectado" under Art. 161-A — [Código Penal](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=1984)
- [P-excerpt] Authorization that covers the recording itself and is "inequívoca" — [Rol 38159-2016, Microjuris](https://aldiachile.microjuris.com/2016/08/16/corte-suprema-acoge-parcialmente-recurso-de-nulidad-de-equipo-periodistico-condenado-por-grabacion-ilegal/)
- [P] Art. 12 (prior, informed, specific, affirmative, revocable), Art. 14 ter (transfers and retention), Art. 16 ter (voice identification) — [Ley 19.628, future version](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-12-01); and today's Art. 4 written consent — [Ley 19.628, current](https://www.leychile.cl/Consulta/obtxml?opt=7&idNorma=141599&idVersion=2026-11-02)

**Al comenzar la reunión (dicho):**
> "Antes de partir, un aviso: me gustaría grabar y transcribir esta reunión con [herramienta], que usa IA, solo para sacar el acta y los pendientes. Queda guardada [X días], la ven solo [quiénes] y el proveedor procesa los datos fuera de Chile. ¿Les parece bien? Necesito un sí de cada uno, acá o en el chat. Si alguien prefiere que no, no grabo y tomo notas a mano, sin ningún problema."

**En la invitación:**
> "Propongo grabar y transcribir esta reunión con [herramienta] (IA), solo para preparar el acta y los compromisos, si todas las personas asistentes están de acuerdo. Los datos los procesa [proveedor] fuera de Chile, se guardan [X] días y solo tendrá acceso [quiénes]. [No se usan para entrenar modelos de IA.] Si prefieres que no se grabe, avísame antes o dilo al comenzar; no necesitas dar motivos. Para pedir una copia o que se borre, escribe a [correo]."

**En el chat, para dejar constancia:**
> "Quiero grabar y transcribir con IA ([herramienta]) solo para el acta. ¿Están de acuerdo? Respondan 'sí' acá; si alguien prefiere que no, no grabo."

**Agregar solo si corresponde:**
- Si la herramienta reconoce voces: "La herramienta identifica a cada persona por su voz; si no quieres eso, dímelo y lo desactivo."
- En contexto laboral, solo si es cierto: "No se usará para evaluar ni sancionar a nadie."

### Inferences
- The invitation is notice and not consent, so still ask at the start. Silence or staying on the call is weak evidence of an affirmative act.
- Include the bracketed no-training sentence only if verified for the plan in use. Under the current vendor terms it would be false by default on Otter, and on Fathom and non-Enterprise Granola unless switched off.
- Do not start recording before the yes. Keep the chat log or a note of who agreed. Pause if someone withdraws, since Art. 12 allows revocation.
- **Have a lawyer confirm before using AI capture in:** covert recording for evidence; HR, disciplinary, harassment or union meetings; employer-wide recording policies; health data, minors or legally privileged talks; voiceprint features; routine transfers of client data abroad once Ley 21.719 applies; and calls with people in all-party US states or the EU where the stakes are high.

### Gaps
- No Chilean regulator or court has approved any wording, so this is a practical draft for a lawyer to adjust. "X días" and access should reflect the tool's real settings.
