# Meeting recorder and AI notetaker apps: free limits, paid pricing and Claude connectors (checked 2026-11-02)

## What does each product offer on free and paid plans, how does it capture, and which plan does its Claude connector need?

### Takeaway
Free-plan value and Claude access rarely line up. On 2026-11-02 the products that combine a free recorder, a Claude connector on the free plan and bot-free capture on Windows are Circleback, Grain, Granola (30-day notes only) and Wispr Flow (weekly cap), plus low-volume options such as Read AI, Otter, MeetGeek and Fireflies; Fathom has the most generous free recorder and a free connector, but on Windows it joins as a visible bot.

### Cited Findings

**How to read the table**
- All vendor pages were loaded on 2026-11-02. Most show no revision date beyond "© 2026", so that date is the check date, not a vendor-confirmed effective date.
- Prices are per user per month, written as monthly billing / annual billing (annual is the per-month figure). "[U]" means unverified: the official page did not show the figure or failed to load.
- "Dir: yes" means the product was returned by the Claude connector registry available in this session, queried on 2026-11-02. "Dir: not returned" means an exact-name search returned nothing relevant, which is weaker than proof of absence (see the conflicts log).
- Claude's own side applies to every row. Directory connectors work for all Claude users, and Free users can add one custom connector ([Claude connectors article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-use-connectors-to-extend-claude-s-capabilities); [Claude custom connectors article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-getting-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp)). Anthropic also warns that the connected service "may require its own account or paid plan" ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/getting-started.md)).

### Inferences
- For Windows 11 Home and mostly Spanish meetings, the strongest free shortlist is Circleback, then Grain for meetings under 45 minutes, then Granola if notes without transcripts are enough. Fathom is the fallback if a visible bot is acceptable, and it needs no new connector setup in this account.

## What are the common objections and failure modes reported by real users?

### Takeaway
Bots are increasingly stopped by the platforms and hosts, while bot-free capture moves the problem to consent and speaker labels. I found no vendor-specific data on Chilean Spanish, and direct Reddit and G2 evidence is missing from these notes.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence limit.** The search tool rejected reddit.com ("not accessible to our user agent"), and the session's web search budget ran out before G2 and Capterra queries ran. The items below are platform documents, press, law-firm notes, one consultant blog and vendor-run tests, not first-hand user threads.

**Bots blocked by platforms and hosts**
- Teams can detect non-Microsoft bots, send them to the lobby and ask the organizer to confirm, "even in meetings where organizers allow participants to bypass the lobby". An analyst notes it does not yet block them outright, and browser extensions and personal devices escape it (July 1, 2026) — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/?p=4191798).
- Google says third-party note-taker bots using "Ask to join" are automatically denied when hosts turn that option off under Trusted or Restricted access. The page does not say whether a calendar-invited bot can still join — [Google help](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/11989526?hl=en).
- Since March 24, 2026, Meet hosts see a second knock queue whose default is to deny, including for personal accounts. The post does not mention bots — [Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/02/safeguarded-guest-admit-flow-in-google-meet.html). A developer forum reports Meet flagging a notetaker as "Potential Risk" — [Nylas forum](https://forums.nylas.com/t/google-meet-flagging-nylas-notetaker-as-potential-risk/1129).

**Consent and legal exposure, both with bots and without**
- In re Otter.AI: on Aug 13, 2026 the Northern District of California let federal wiretap, California privacy and Illinois biometric claims proceed. No liability has been decided — [UC Today](https://uctoday.com/productivity-automation/otter-ai-fails-to-dismiss-core-privacy-claims-in-u-s-court/).
